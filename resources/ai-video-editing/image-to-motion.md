# From a starting image to animated B-roll

First decide what the viewer should understand from the shot. Then choose a composition that makes the action easy to read.

## Starting-image brief

```text
Purpose in the video: [what this shot should communicate].
Subject and environment: [who or what, and where].
Composition: [framing and placement].
Visual style: [reference, colors, materials or illustration treatment].
Starting pose: [the state before the action begins].
Leave room for: [captions or other required content].
```

Inspect the image before animating it. Check the pose, props, framing and consistency with the other shots.

## Motion brief

```text
Use the supplied approved image as the starting frame.
Action: [one clear action with a beginning and end].
Keep consistent: [subject, clothing, props, palette and composition].
Camera: [locked or a deliberate movement].
Duration and format: [choose options the selected model supports].
Audio: [what this shot actually needs].
Avoid: [unwanted new objects, cuts, text or changes].
```

For example: a person reaches for a drink, takes a small sip and puts it back, while the camera and room stay consistent.

You can use this worksheet when creating motion in Higgsfield. Check the actual available model/settings and inspect the generated result. Prompts describe intent; they do not guarantee motion, identity or timing. Once accepted, bring the clip into the edit and review it with the narration.
